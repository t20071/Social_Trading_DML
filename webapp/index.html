1 · The data

Self-trading happens in bursts

Each bar is one week (a "period"). Self-trading fades after mid-June, almost vanishes for four months, then explodes in the last three weeks. Those three weeks hold two-thirds of all self-trades.

2 · One number, three kinds of uncertainty

How sure are we about −5.44?

Trader-clustered intervals assume traders are independent within a week. Market-wide shocks break that, and the variation comes from only about four effective weeks. Switch the inference and watch the intervals grow.

3 · Where the estimate comes from

A weighted average of two regimes

The pooled estimate is exactly a weighted average of week-by-week contributions. Change the weighting and the "answer" moves from −5 to −13. Hover a week to see what happens without it.

4 · What the effect consists of

The estimate is the P&L of the marginal trade

Each dot is a week with at least 100 self-trades. On the x-axis is the raw average P&L per self-trade, straight from the data with no model. On the y-axis is the model's week contribution. They line up.

5 · Heterogeneity on held-out traders

"Different traders" turn out to be different weeks

A causal forest was fit on half the traders and tested on the other half. Its quartiles differ sharply, but the colour shows why: the less-negative quartiles trade almost entirely in the final three weeks.

7 · Identification

Strict exogeneity fails

Next week's self-trades predict this week's gains, consistent with gains feeding back into later trading. The estimator that stays valid under this feedback (Anderson–Hsiao, instrumented by self-trades two weeks earlier) cannot tell the sign.
